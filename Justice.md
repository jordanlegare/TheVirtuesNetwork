# [[Justice]]

[[Justice]] is the second of the four **Cardinal virtues** (with [[Prudence]], [[Fortitude]], and [[Temperance]]). It is **giving each their due** and **treating equals equally**. Justice is not merely legalistic fairness but **right relationship** between all persons and things [1][2][3].

> _"Justice binds men to one another in a bond of concord, and is that virtue that disposes each individual to render to God and neighbor the rights which pertain to them by reason of reason and of nature."_

## Core Definition

[[Justice]] is the virtue of **fairness** and **right relationship**. It ensures each person receives what is due: respect, love, fairness, and what is owed. Justice is giving each their due, treating equals equally and unequals unequally according to their needs.

## The Cardinal Virtues

### [[Prudence]] - Wise judgment
- [[Prudence]] discerns what is fair to each person
- [[Justice]] applies [[Prudence]'s guidance to specific cases

### [[Fortitude]] - Strength in injustice
- [[Fortitude]] enables us to stand firm against injustice
- [[Justice]] provides the moral strength to resist unfairness

### [[Temperance]] - Moderation in relationships
- [[Temperance]] prevents excess and deficiency in giving
- [[Justice]] applies [[Temperance]] to fair measure

## The Social Hierarchy and Justice

[[Justice]] operates in the social hierarchy, giving each their due according to position and need:

- **Rulers**: Give wise and fair leadership
- **Soldiers**: Provide defense and protection
- **Peasants**: Tend the land with diligence
- **Artisans**: Create with skill and care
- **Scholars**: Study and teach truth
- **Priests**: Lead in faith and worship
- **Everyone**: Practice their calling with virtue

Each position requires [[Justice]] to give what is due. The social order flourishes when each does [[Justice]] in their role [2][3].

## Justice and the Fruits of the Spirit

The fruits of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 13:13) include [[Charity]], faith, and hope:

- **[[Charity]]**: Justice is perfected by love
- **Faith**: Trusts that God is just
- **[[Hope]]**: Expects ultimate justice from God
- **[[Patience]]**: Endures unjust treatment
- **[[Diligence]]**: Works for justice consistently

See [[Charity]] and the other seven heavenly virtues [2][3].

## Barriers to Justice

- **Injustice**: Unfairness in our own hearts
- **Unfairness**: Partiality toward friends or enemies
- **Vice**: Vices cloud our sense of justice
- **Selfishness**: Wanting advantage for ourselves
- **Anger**: Emotional injustice toward wrongdoers
- **Fear**: Paralysis before difficult decisions
- **Ignorance**: Not knowing what justice requires
- **Prejudice**: Bias distorts justice

Remove these barriers through virtue cultivation and practice [2][3].

## Justice in the Tradition

The four Cardinal virtues come from Plato's [[Republic]] and were systematized by the Stoics. They were carried into Christian theology by Ambrose and Aquinas.

[[Justice]] is one of the [[Cardinal]] virtues with [[Prudence]], [[Fortitude]], and [[Temperance]]. The three theological virtues [[Faith]], [[Hope]], and [[Charity]] are different: they are given, not built [1][2].
