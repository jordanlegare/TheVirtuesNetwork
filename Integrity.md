# [[Integrity]]

[[Integrity]] is the virtue of **moral wholeness** and consistency in all things. It is the capacity to remain true to our convictions and moral principles regardless of circumstances, pressure, or convenience. [[Integrity]] is not merely doing what is right but being the kind of person who cannot do wrong. [[Integrity]] is distinct from [[Truthfulness]] but includes it as its expression. [[Integrity]] says: "Whatever I say and do will be in accordance with what I believe is right." [1][2][3].

> _"Integrity is the virtue of maintaining moral consistency, doing right regardless of circumstances, and being the same person inwardly as outwardly. Integrity is being a person in whom you can trust."_

## Core Definition

[[Integrity]] is the virtue of **moral wholeness** and moral unity. It is the capacity to be the same person in private and public, to do right regardless of consequences, and to remain true to convictions despite pressure to compromise. [[Integrity]] is not merely a set of rules but a **character trait** that makes us do right naturally.

[[Integrity]] is the opposite of duplicity and hypocrisy, which involves two-faced people who act different from who they are. [[Integrity]] means being the person you say you are, doing what you say you will do, and letting others know who you really are. [[Integrity]] is a moral strength that requires courage and faithfulness [1][2][3].

## The Wholeness

[[Integrity]] operates as moral wholeness:

1. **Unity**: Unity of heart, mind, and action
2. **Truthfulness**: Being truthful in all things
3. **Consistency**: Consistent in character
4. **Faithfulness**: Faithful to commitments
5. **Honesty**: Honest in all dealings
6. **Reliability**: Reliable and trustworthy
7. **Responsibility**: Take responsibility for actions
8. **Accountability**: Accountable for decisions
9. **Integrity**: Maintain moral principles
10. **Character**: Character built on right principles

## Barriers to Integrity

- **Duplicity**: Being two-faced
- **Hypocrisy**: Saying is one thing, doing another
- **Deception**: Deliberate deception
- **Worldly**: Worldly compromises
- **Fear**: Fear of losing something
- **Profit**: Profit at cost of integrity
- **Pressure**: Pressure to compromise
- **Convenience**: Convenience over right
- **Selfishness**: Selfishness over integrity
- **Apathy**: Not caring about integrity

Remove these barriers through [[Integrity]] practice and [[Humility]] [3][4].

## Integrity in the Tradition

[[Integrity]] is the virtue of moral wholeness and consistency. Jesus said, "Let your light so shine before men that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven" (Matthew 5:16). [[Pope Gregory X]] lists [[Integrity]] in the Rule of Wisdom as a central virtue for our moral wholeness. The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) and the three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are the framework for our spiritual growth. The seven heavenly virtues (with [[Humility]], [[Charity]], [[Chastity]], [[Kindness]], [[Patience]], [[Diligence]], [[Temperance]]) complete our moral life [1][2][3].
