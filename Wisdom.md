# [[Wisdom]]

[[Wisdom]] is the virtue of **discernment** and **right judgment**. It is the capacity to see life holistically, to understand what matters and why, and to apply right reason to action. [[Wisdom]] is not merely intelligence or knowledge but the **practical application** of knowledge toward the good. [[Wisdom]] is sometimes called **metiscian wisdom** (the wisdom of life experience) rather than philosophical wisdom [1][2][3].

> _"Wisdom is the virtue that enables us to see what truly matters, to understand the connections between things, and to apply practical reason to action toward the good."_

## Core Definition

[[Wisdom]] is the virtue of **practical judgment** and **right perspective**. It is the capacity to see the whole picture, to recognize the importance of various goods, and to apply our knowledge and experience toward real good. [[Wisdom]] distinguishes what is essential from what is peripheral, what is temporary from what is eternal, and what truly matters from what we falsely think matters.

[[Wisdom]] is the crown of the virtues: it directs [[Prudence]] to the right end, guides [[Charity]] appropriately, and ensures other virtues are applied correctly. [[Wisdom]] is the ability to see clearly what God wants from us and to act accordingly. [[Wisdom]] is not a separate virtue from the virtues but the **perfection** of all virtues [1][2][3].

## The Crown of Virtues

[[Wisdom]] is the crowning perfection of all virtues:

1. **Charity**: [[Wisdom]] directs [[Charity]] to the right end
2. **Prudence**: [[Wisdom]] guides [[Prudence]] to the right mean
3. **Justice**: [[Wisdom]] applies [[Justice]] wisely
4. **Fortitude**: [[Wisdom]] directs [[Fortitude]] appropriately
5. **Temperance**: [[Wisdom]] guides [[Temperance]] to moderation
6. **Faith**: [[Wisdom]] supports [[Faith]] with understanding
7. **Hope**: [[Wisdom]] strengthens [[Hope]] with knowledge
8. **Charity**: [[Wisdom]] perfects [[Charity]] with love

See the fruits of the Spirit and the Cardinal virtues [2][3].

## Daily Life Application

### In Decision-Making
- **Reflect**: Consider all factors carefully
- **Seek Truth**: Pursue truth before acting
- **Consider Consequences**: Think through outcomes
- **Consult Wise Counsel**: Seek advice from those with wisdom
- **Pray for Wisdom**: Ask God for right judgment
- **Apply Experience**: Learn from what has happened

### In Relationships
- **Listen Wisely**: Understand before responding
- **Forgive**: Release grudges to live in peace
- **Encourage**: Build others' capacity for good
- **Resolve Conflict**: Seek peace and reconciliation
- **Serve**: Help without expecting recognition

### In Self-Growth
- **Study**: Learn and reflect on truth
- **Reflect**: Think about daily life experiences
- **Pray**: Ask for increase in wisdom
- **Associate**: Spend time with wise people
- **Write**: Journal and organize thoughts

## Practical Wisdom Practices

1. **Daily Reflection**: End each day reviewing decisions
2. **Scripture Study**: Learn from divine wisdom
3. **Reading**: Study works on wisdom and ethics
4. **Mentorship**: Seek wise mentors and offer mentorship
5. **Journaling**: Record insights and learnings
6. **Prayer**: Ask for increase in [[Wisdom]]
7. **Observation**: Watch wise people in action
8. **Teaching**: Share what you know to solidify understanding

## The Three Modes of Wisdom

[[Wisdom]] operates in three ways:

1. **Theoretical**: Understanding truth itself (scientia)
2. **Practical**: Applying knowledge to action (prudence)
3. **Contemplative**: Seeing unity in all things (sapientia)

See [[Prudence]] and the fruits of the Spirit [2][3].

## Barriers to Wisdom

- **Ignorance**: Not seeking truth
- **Stubbornness**: Refusing to learn
- **Pride**: Thinking we know better than we do
- **Sloth**: Not putting in effort to learn
- **Fear**: Fear of being wrong or wronged
- **Worldliness**: Not caring about eternal truths
- **Skepticism**: Dismissing without examining
- **Prejudice**: Judging before understanding

Remove these barriers through study, reflection, community, and humility [3][4].

## Wisdom in the Tradition

[[Pope Gregory X]] in his "Rule of Wisdom" lists four degrees or levels of knowledge:

1. **Science** - Knowing the truth itself
2. **Art** - Applying truth to action
3. **Knowledge** - Understanding causes and relations
4. **Experience** - Knowing from practice and reflection

[[Wisdom]] is the perfection of all these forms. The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) and the three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are the framework for our spiritual growth [1][2][3].
