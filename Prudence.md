# [[Prudence]]

[[Prudence]] is the first of the four **Cardinal virtues** (with [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], and [[Temperance]]). It is **practical wisdom** (phronesis) and **discernment** of the right means to right ends. Prudence is not cunning but **wise judgment** that guides us through complex situations with clarity and good sense [1][2][3].

> _"Prudence is the royal virtue which directs us in our actions and enables us to apply truth to life."_

## Core Definition

[[Prudence]] is the virtue of **wise judgment** and **right reason** in action. It is the capacity to see clearly the nature of a situation, weigh competing considerations, and discern the best course. Prudence teaches us to ask the right questions, gather the right information, and choose the right action.

## Interconnection Network

### With [[Beauty]]
- [[Beauty]] is made practical through [[Prudence]]. Wise actions are beautiful actions.
- [[Prudence]] discerns the beautiful action in each circumstance.
- **Prudence + Beauty** = Action that is both right and good

### With [[Grace]]
- [[Grace]] enables [[Prudence]] in difficult judgments.
- [[Prudence]] knows when [[Grace]] calls for mercy over justice.
- **Prudence + Grace** = Judged with wisdom and mercy

### With [[Elegance]]
- [[Elegance]] is the appropriate application of [[Prudence]].
- [[Prudence]] knows what is fitting and proper.
- **Prudence + Elegance** = Appropriate action

### With [[Harmony]]
- [[Prudence]] seeks harmony in all decisions.
- [[Harmony]] is maintained through prudent balance.
- **Prudence + Harmony** = Balanced wisdom

### With [[Nature]]
- [[Nature]] teaches [[Prudence]] through observation and learning.
- [[Prudence]] learns natural patterns and cycles.
- **Prudence + Nature** = Wisdom from observation

### With [[Care]]
- [[Care]] requires [[Prudence]] to know what to care for.
- [[Prudence]] directs our care to where it's needed most.
- **Prudence + Care** = Attentive compassion

### With [[Experience]]
- [[Experience]] builds [[Prudence]] through reflection on the past.
- [[Prudence]] applies lessons from [[Experience]] to new situations.
- **Prudence + Experience** = Wisdom in action

### With [[Flow]]
- [[Flow]] requires [[Prudence]] to know the right moment to act.
- [[Prudence]] discerns when to enter or leave flow.
- **Prudence + Flow** = Timely action

### With [[Giving]]
- [[Giving]] is prudent when [[Prudence]] guides what we can afford.
- [[Prudence]] avoids giving that harms giver or receiver.
- **Prudence + Giving** = Sustained generosity

## The Cardinal Virtues

### [[Justice]] - Giving each their due
- [[Justice]] applies [[Prudence]] to fair distribution.
- [[Prudence]] discerns what each person deserves.

### [[Fortitude]] - Courage to do the right thing
- [[Fortitude]] gives the strength to act on [[Prudence]]'s judgments.
- [[Prudence]] guides [[Fortitude]] toward the right action.

### [[Temperance]] - Moderation in action
- [[Prudence]] knows how much is enough and how to moderate.
- [[Temperance]] ensures [[Prudence]]'s action is measured.

## Daily Life Application

### Decision-Making
- **Pause and reflect**: Take time to think before acting
- **Gather information**: Know what you need before deciding
- **Consult wisely**: Seek counsel from those with experience
- **Consider consequences**: Think through likely outcomes
- **Apply virtue's guidance**: Use the Cardinal and Theological virtues to guide
- **Act decisively**: Once decided, commit to the course

### When Facing Moral Complexity
- **Ask**: What is the right thing to do?
- **Recall**: What would a prudent person do?
- **Consider**: How does this affect others?
- **Pray**: Ask for wisdom and clarity
- **Decide**: Choose the prudent course
- **Act**: Do what's right

## Practical Prudence Practices

1. **Daily Reflection**: End each day reviewing decisions made
2. **Wisdom Reading**: Study works on prudence and virtue ethics
3. **Mentorship**: Seek wise counsel and offer it to others
4. **Observation**: Watch prudent people in action
5. **Gratitude**: Thank those who guide your judgment
6. **Examination**: Review decisions for better choices

## The Three Acts of Virtue

[[Prudence]] operates through three acts:

1. **Counsel (consultation)**: Seek and hear wise opinions
2. **Deliberation (reflection)**: Weigh options and consider consequences
3. **Decision (rectitude)**: Willingly choose and commit to the right action

## Prudence and the Fruits of the Spirit

The fruits of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 13:13) include [[Charity|Charity]], faith, and hope:

- **[[Charity]]**: Prudence directs love toward right ends
- **Faith**: Prudence guides belief to reasonable assent
- **[[Hope]]**: Prudence helps maintain prudent hope

See [[Charity]], [[Faith]], [[Hope]] and the seven deadly sins [2][3].

## Barriers to Prudence

- **Haste**: Acting without due deliberation
- **Ignorance**: Not knowing relevant facts
- **Passions**: Emotions clouding judgment
- **Vice**: Habitual patterns distorting view
- **Presumption**: Assuming we know better than others
- **Advisory isolation**: Refusing counsel
- **Inexperience**: Not enough reflection on past actions

Remove these barriers through self-awareness, humility, and practice [2][3].

## Prudence in the Tradition

The four Cardinal virtues come from Plato's [[Republic]] and were systematized by the Stoics. They were carried into Christian theology by Ambrose and Aquinas.

**Three theological virtues** come from 1 Corinthians 13:13: [[Faith]], [[Hope]], and [[Charity]]. Where the lists cross is the whole point: Temperance sits on both the cardinal and remedial rolls, and [[Charity]] is at once a remedy and the greatest of the theological gifts [1][2].
