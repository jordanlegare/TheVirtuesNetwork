# [[Peace]]

[[Peace]] is the fruit of the Spirit that fills our hearts with tranquility, removing the tumult of anxiety, anger, and disturbance. [[Peace]] is not merely absence of conflict but **positive peace**: the presence of God in our hearts and the harmony of right relationships. [[Peace]] is distinct from [[Harmony]] which refers to relationship balance, but [[Peace]] is tranquility within ourselves and right relationships in all things [1][2][3].

> _"Peace is the fruit of the Spirit that fills our hearts with tranquility. Peace is not merely absence of conflict but the presence of God in our hearts and right relationships with all things."_

## Core Definition

[[Peace]] is the virtue of **tranquility** and contentment in God's sovereignty. It is not a forced calm but a tranquil peace that flows from trusting God's goodness and justice. [[Peace]] is the capacity to remain calm and trusting in difficult circumstances, knowing that God is at work and that all things work together for good.

[[Peace]] is the opposite of anxiety, which disrupts our tranquility and distracts us from God. [[Peace]] is not passive but **actively maintained** through trust in God, prayer, and faithful living. [[Peace]] brings us a tranquility that calms the tumult of our hearts [1][2][3].

## The Two Forms of Peace

[[Peace]] operates in two ways:

1. **Inner Peace**: Tranquility within our hearts
2. **Outward Peace**: Peaceful relationships with others

[[Peace]] is both inner and outward tranquility [3][4].

## Barriers to Peace

- **Anxiety**: Fear for the future
- **Anger**: Unresolved anger disrupts [[Peace]]
- **Worry**: Worry over circumstances
- **Worldly**: Worrying about worldly things
- **Fear**: Fear of what might happen
- **Control**: Trying to control what cannot be controlled
- **Worldly**: Worldly worries
- **Guilt**: Unconfessed sin
- **Unforgiveness**: Holding grudges
- **Pride**: Not trusting God's sovereignty

Remove these barriers through prayer, forgiveness, and [[Humility]] [3][4].

## Peace in the Tradition

[[Peace]] is the fruit of the Spirit that fills our hearts with tranquility. Jesus taught his disciples to "have peace" (John 14:27), promising his own peace. [[Pope Gregory X]] lists [[Peace]] in the Rule of Wisdom as a central virtue for our peace and tranquility. The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) and the three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are the framework for our spiritual growth. The seven heavenly virtues (with [[Humility]], [[Charity]], [[Chastity]], [[Kindness]], [[Patience]], [[Diligence]], [[Temperance]]) complete our moral life [1][2][3].
