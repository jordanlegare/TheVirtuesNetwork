# [[Kindness]]

[[Kindness]] is one of the seven **heavenly virtues** (the seven remedial virtues, one counter-practice per seven deadly sin). [[Kindness]] counters **envy** and teaches us to rejoice in others' good fortune and seek the good of others for their own sake. [[Kindness]] is the opposite of envy, not just an emotion but a **virtuous habit** of desiring the blessing of others [1][2][3].

> _"Kindness is the virtue of desiring and doing good to others without expectation of reward, motivated by the love of God and neighbor."_

## Core Definition

[[Kindness]] is the virtue of **benevolence** and **compassionate action**. It desires the good of others and is willing to do whatever is needed to bring about that good. [[Kindness]] is not merely feeling sympathy but **active benevolence** toward others. [[Kindness]] says: "I want you to have what is good for you and will help you achieve it."

[[Kindness]] is the fruit of the Spirit that motivates us to do good to others without seeking recognition or reward. [[Kindness]] flows from the love of God to our neighbors. [[Kindness]] is the practical expression of [[Charity]] in everyday interactions [1][2][3].

## The Seven Heavenly Virtues

The seven heavenly virtues (the seven remedial virtues, one counter-practice per seven deadly sin) are:

1. **[[Humility]]** - Against Pride
2. **[[Charity]]** - Against Greed/Avarice
3. **[[Chastity]]** - Against Lust
4. **[[Kindness]]** - Against Envy
5. **[[Patience]]** - Against Wrath
6. **[[Diligence]]** - Against Sloth
7. **[[Temperance]]** - Against Gluttony

See all seven virtues with their respective vices [3][4].

## Daily Life Application

### In Relationships
- **Encourage**: Support others' growth
- **Help**: Assist without expecting return
- **Comfort**: Listen and care for those in pain
- **Forgive**: Release grudges quickly
- **Smile**: Welcome others genuinely

### In Community
- **Serve**: Help neighbors without recognition
- **Share**: Offer what you have freely
- **Welcome**: Accept newcomers warmly
- **Support**: Champion others' opportunities
- **Reconcile**: Restore broken bonds

### In Work and Life
- **Be Considerate**: Think of others' needs
- **Be Helpful**: Assist without complaint
- **Be Encouraging**: Uplift colleagues and friends
- **Be Forgiving**: Let go of grudges quickly
- **Be Generous**: Give time and resources freely

## Practical Kindness Practices

1. **Daily Acts**: Perform one act of kindness daily
2. **Listen**: Really hear others' needs
3. **Volunteer**: Serve in community projects
4. **Share**: Offer resources freely
5. **Encourage**: Compliment and uplift others
6. **Forgive**: Release grudges deliberately
7. **Welcome**: Make others feel at home
8. **Pray**: For those who are different

## The Fruits of the Spirit

The fruits of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 13:13) are connected to [[Kindness]]:

1. **[[Joy]]** - Contentment in others' well-being
2. **[[Peace]]** - Harmony through [[Kindness]]
3. **[[Patience]]** - Enduring others patiently
4. **[[Gentleness]]** - Treating others gently
5. **[[Goodness]]** - Doing good to others
6. **[[Fidelity]]** - Faithful relationships
7. **[[Self-Control]]** - Controlling envy and jealousy
8. **[[Humility]]** - Serving without pride

See [[Charity]] and the other fruits of the Spirit [2][3].

## Barriers to Kindness

- **Envy**: Wanting what others have
- **Jealousy**: Wanting to possess others
- **Resentment**: Bitterness at others' success
- **Competition**: Wanting to surpass others
- **Comparison**: Measuring against others
- **Cynicism**: Believing others only serve themselves
- **Selfishness**: Prioritizing self over others
- **Fear**: Fear of being taken advantage of

Remove these barriers through [[Grace]] and practice [3][4].

## Kindness in the Tradition

[[Kindness]] is the fruit of the Spirit that makes us desire the good of others. The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) and the three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are the framework for our spiritual growth. The seven heavenly virtues complete the picture [1][2][3].
