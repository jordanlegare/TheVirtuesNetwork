# [[Fortitude]]

[[Fortitude]] is the third of the four **Cardinal virtues** (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], and [[Temperance]]). It is **moral courage** and **strength** to endure adversity and stand firm in truth and goodness despite fear and difficulty. Fortitude is not recklessness but **courageous firmness** in doing what is right [1][2][3].

> _"Fortitude is the virtue that enables us to endure hardship with courage and strength, standing firm in adversity and resisting temptation."_

## Core Definition

[[Fortitude]] is the virtue of **courage** and **endurance**. It gives us the strength to face fear, endure suffering, and persevere in doing good despite difficulty. Fortitude is the moral strength that enables us to act according to [[Prudence]] even when it is difficult or dangerous, to maintain [[Justice]] despite opposition, and to uphold virtue even under pressure.

## The Cardinal Virtues

### [[Prudence]] - Wise judgment
- [[Fortitude]] follows [[Prudence]'s guidance to do right
- [[Prudence]] directs [[Fortitude]] to the right action

### [[Justice]] - Acting justly
- [[Fortitude]] gives strength to do [[Justice]]
- [[Justice]] gives purpose to [[Fortitude]]'s strength

### [[Temperance]] - Moderate courage
- [[Temperance]] prevents [[Fortitude]] becoming recklessness
- [[Fortitude]] ensures [[Temperance]] is supported by strength

## Daily Life Application

### When Facing Adversity
- **Endure**: Keep going despite hardship
- **Persist**: Don't quit despite difficulty
- **Trust**: Trust that all things work together for good
- **Pray**: Ask for strength to endure
- **Remember**: Past trials have brought good fruit

### When Feeling Fearful
- **Face**: Don't avoid what you fear
- **Pray**: Ask for courage to proceed
- **Act**: Do the right thing despite fear
- **Endure**: Keep going even when difficult
- **Trust**: Trust in ultimate good

### When Persevering
- **Continue**: Keep doing the right thing
- **Endure**: Stand firm against opposition
- **Maintain**: Hold to virtue despite temptation
- **Persist**: Don't give up easily
- **Endure**: Keep striving toward virtue

## Practical Fortitude Practices

1. **Face Fears**: Confront fears head-on
2. **Pray for Courage**: Ask God for strength
3. **Remember Past Victory**: Recall times when you succeeded

## The Four Trials of Virtue

[[Fortitude]] must endure four trials:

1. **Labor**: Hard work and effort
2. **Adversity**: Difficult circumstances
3. **Need**: Personal lack and want
4. **Death**: The ultimate challenge

[[Fortitude]] is tested and strengthened through these trials [2][3].

## Barriers to Fortitude

- **Fear**: Paralyzes courage
- **Pride**: Refuses help or admission of need
- **Laziness**: Avoids necessary endurance
- **Weakness**: Lack of moral strength
- **Despair**: Giving up as futile
- **Anger**: Emotional rage overwhelms reason
- **Fear**: Avoidance of danger or hardship

Remove these barriers through virtue cultivation and acceptance of help when needed [2][3].

## Fortitude in the Tradition

The four Cardinal virtues come from Plato's [[Republic]] and were systematized by the Stoics. They were carried into Christian theology by Ambrose and Aquinas.

[[Fortitude]] is one of the [[Cardinal]] virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Temperance]] to the [[Temptation of the Three Spheres]]). The three theological virtues [[Faith]], [[Hope]], and [[Charity]] are different: they are infused, not acquired [1][2].

## The Fortitude Virtue Cycle

1. **Fear** arises
2. **[[Fortitude]]** strengthens courage
3. **Courage** enables right action
4. **Endurance** overcomes difficulty
5. **Victory** strengthens [[Fortitude]]
6. **[[Fortitude]]** grows stronger and more confident
7. **Cycle continues**: Greater courage in greater trials
