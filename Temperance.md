# [[Temperance]]

[[Temperance]] is the fourth of the four **Cardinal virtues** (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], and [[Fortitude]]). It is also the **seventh of the seven heavenly virtues** (the seven remedial virtues). [[Temperance]] is **self-mastery and moderation** over pleasures and desires. It is the capacity to control our appetites and find freedom in not being enslaved by impulses [1][2][3].

> _"Temperance is the moral virtue that moderates the attraction of pleasures and provides balance in the use of created goods. It is the opposite of gluttony, the sin against which it defends particularly but not only the sense of taste, but also of sight and smell; of sexuality; and of temperance in acquiring money and using it."_ — Catechism of the Catholic Church

## Core Definition

[[Temperance]] is the virtue of **moderation** and **self-mastery**. It enables us to control our impulses, use our strengths without excess, and find satisfaction in not always needing stimulation. [[Temperance]] is not mere repression but the **rational ordering** of our appetites toward the good. [[Temperance]] teaches us that freedom is not license to do whatever we want but the capacity to control ourselves without compulsion.

## Daily Life Application

### Controlling Appetites
- **Recognize**: Notice when you're seeking excess
- **Evaluate**: Ask if this is needed or just craving
- **Moderate**: Use without excess or deficiency
- **Restrain**: Say no to unnecessary pleasures
- **Practice**: Make moderation a habit

### Managing Anger
- **Endure**: Bear slights without retaliating
- **Forgive**: Grant mercy to others
- **Reconcile**: Restore relationships
- **Accept**: Accept necessary suffering
- **Endure**: Keep trying despite failures

### Managing Envy
- **Acknowledge**: Recognize envy when present
- **Gratitude**: Focus on what you have
- **Give**: Give to those in need
- **Support**: Encourage rather than envy others
- **Rejoice**: Glory over others' good fortune

## Practical Temperance Practices

1. **Mindful Consumption**: Think before consuming anything
2. **Saying No**: Learn to refuse excess
3. **Fasting**: Practice abstinence voluntarily
4. **Prayer**: Ask for strength to resist temptation
5. **Gratitude**: Appreciate what you have
6. **Simplicity**: Live with less need
7. **Rest**: Allow for rest and recovery
8. **Solace**: Find satisfaction in non-possession

- **[[Fidelity]]**: Stays faithful
- **[[Gentleness]]**: Deals gently with others
- **[[Self-Control]]**: Mastery over passions

See [[Charity]] and the other fruits of the Spirit [3][4].

## Barriers to Temperance

- **Gluttony**: Excessive indulgence in pleasures
- **Addiction**: Slavery to substances or activities
- **Compulsion**: Unable to control appetites
- **Lack of Will**: Unable to exercise self-control
- **Desire**: Always wanting more
- **Pain**: Seeking distraction from discomfort
- **Vice**: Habituated behavior

Remove these barriers through practice, prayer, and seeking accountability [3][4].

## Temperance in the Tradition

The four Cardinal virtues come from Plato's [[Republic]] and were systematized by the Stoics. They were carried into Christian theology by Ambrose and Aquinas.

The seven heavenly virtues are the seven remedial virtues, one counter-practice per seven deadly sins. [[Temperance]] sits on both the [[Cardinal]] and remedial rolls: it is a moral habit and the medicine matched to gluttony [1][2][3].
