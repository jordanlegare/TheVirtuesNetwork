# [[Hope]]

[[Hope]] is one of the three **Theological virtues** (with [[Faith]] and [[Charity]]). It comes from 1 Corinthians 13:13: "[[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]], and love is abiding." [[Hope]] is the theological virtue by which we **hope in God's promises**, **expect what he has promised**, and **look forward** with trust and confidence to eternal life. [[Hope]] directs us toward the future good [1][2][3].

> _"Hope is the theological virtue by which we desire God, who is immortal and all-good, and place our trust in his promises and counting on his aid and his almighty power."_

## Core Definition

[[Hope]] is the virtue of **trust in the future good**. It is not naive optimism but **confident expectation** of what God has promised. [[Hope]] directs our hearts toward heavenly fulfillment and gives us strength to endure present difficulties. [[Hope]] says: "I may not fully understand, but I trust that God is good and that all things work together for good."

[[Hope]] is the second of the theological virtues to be infused into us by God. It follows [[Faith]]'s trust in what God has revealed, and it enables us to look forward to the fulfillment of God's promises [1][2][3].

## The Theological Virtues

The three theological virtues come from 1 Corinthians 13:13:

1. **[[Faith]]** - Trust in God
2. **[[Hope]]** - Expectation of good from God
3. **[[Charity]]** - Love for God and neighbor

[[Charity]] is the "greatest" because it perfects and unifies the other two [2][3].

## Daily Life Application

### In Difficult Times
- **Trust**: Even when things seem hopeless
- **Expect**: That God is working for good
- **Endure**: Keep going despite present suffering
- **Anticipate**: The glory to come
- **Perservere**: Continue despite temptation

### In Times of Need
- **Pray**: Ask for increase in [[Hope]]
- **Trust**: That God hears our prayers
- **Expect**: That God will provide
- **Endure**: While waiting on God's timing
- **Anticipate**: The fulfillment of promises

### In Community Life
- **Encourage**: Build [[Hope]] in others
- **Support**: Those who struggle with hopelessness
- **Teach**: What we trust in
- **Share**: Stories of hope realized
- **Witness**: To God's goodness

## Practical Hope Practices

1. **Prayer for Hope**: Regular prayer asking for confidence
2. **Scripture Reading**: Meditate on biblical promises
3. **Gratitude**: Remember past blessings
4. **Community**: Encourage others in [[Hope]]
5. **Study**: Learn about God's faithfulness
6. **Service**: Suffering done in hope
7. **Fasting**: Discipline in hope of God's provision
8. **Worship**: Adore God's promises

## Hope and the Fruits of the Spirit

The fruits of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 13:13) are:

- **[[Charity]]**: Love which hope awaits
- **[[Faith]]**: Trust which hope depends on
- **[[Joy]]**: Delight in hopeful confidence
- **[[Peace]]**: Trust in God's plan
- **[[Patience]]**: Enduring in hopeful trust
- **[[Kindness]]**: Hopeful compassion for others
- **[[Goodness]]**: Acting from hopeful trust
- **[[Fidelity]]**: Faithful to God's promises
- **[[Gentleness]]**: Gentle hope for others
- **[[Self-Control]]**: Self-discipline from hopeful trust

See [[Charity]], [[Faith]] and the other fruits of the Spirit [2][3].

## Barriers to Hope

- **Despair**: Believing God will not come through
- **Cynicism**: Expecting the worst from others
- **Fear**: Fear of God's power
- **Worldly Attachment**: Trusting in material things instead of God
- **Anger**: Resisting God's goodness
- **Pride**: Relying on self rather than God
- **Worldliness**: Not caring about eternal things
- **Ignorance**: Not understanding God's promises

Remove these barriers through prayer, sacraments, and community [2][3].

## Hope in the Tradition

The three theological virtues come from 1 Corinthians 13:13. [[Hope]] is the second of these virtues, following [[Faith]] and preceding [[Charity]] in order of acquisition but not in greatness. [[Faith]] and [[Hope]] are like a ladder; [[Charity]] is [[Hope]]'s fulfillment [1][2][3].

The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) are acquired through practice and teaching. The three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are infused by God and cannot be acquired but must be received through grace [2][3].
