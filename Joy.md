# [[Joy]]

[[Joy]] is the fruit of the Spirit that fills our hearts with gladness and good cheer, not dependent on external circumstances but flowing from union with God. [[Joy]] is distinct from [[Happiness]] which is fleeting based on circumstances, but is the **stable gladness** from the Holy Spirit's presence in our lives. [[Joy]] is the antidote to despair and the sign [[Charity]]'s love for God and neighbor [1][2][3].

> _"Joy is the gladness of the Holy Spirit dwelling in us, bringing gladness to our hearts regardless of circumstances. Joy is the stable gladness that flows from our relationship with God._"

## Core Definition

[[Joy]] is the virtue of **inner gladness** and contentment that flows from union with God. It is not merely happiness based on circumstances but a **stable gladness** from the Holy Spirit dwelling in us. [[Joy]] is the capacity to rejoice in all things—giving us gladness even in suffering and trials—and being a source of gladness to others.

[[Joy]] is the antidote to despair and the fruit of [[Charity]]'s love. [[Joy]] is not naive or forced but the **overflow** of our relationship with God. [[Joy]] says: "God is with me, and my heart rejoices in Him regardless of circumstances" [1][2][3].

## Barriers to Joy

- **Despair**: Believing God does not care
- **Anger**: Resentment against God or others
- **Worldly**: Worldly pursuits that bring no joy
- **Anxiety**: Worry about future
- **Pride**: Not trusting God
- **Greed**: Not trusting God's provision
- **Fear**: Fear destroys gladness
- **Pain**: Suffering that we cannot bear in gladness
- **Worldliness**: Loving the world more than God

Remove these barriers through prayer, community, and [[Patience]] [3][4].

## Joy in the Tradition

[[Joy]] is the fruit of the Spirit that fills our hearts with gladness. [[Pope Gregory X]] lists glad heart as a virtue in the Rule of Wisdom. The four Cardinal virtues (with [[Prudence]], [[Justice]], [[Fortitude]], [[Temperance]]) and the three theological virtues (with [[Faith]], [[Hope]], [[Charity]]) are the framework for our spiritual growth. The seven heavenly virtues (with [[Humility]], [[Charity]], [[Chastity]], [[Kindness]], [[Patience]], [[Diligence]], [[Temperance]]) complete our moral life [1][2][3].
